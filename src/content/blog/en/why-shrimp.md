When we think of farmed animals, we think of cows, pigs and chickens. Rarely of shrimp. And yet they are the most numerous of all.

## Mind-boggling numbers

Rethink Priorities estimates that around **230 billion shrimp** are alive on farms at any given moment: more than any other farmed animal. According to Rethink Priorities, around **440 billion** farmed shrimp are killed every year, nearly 14,000 every second. And that's without counting wild-caught shrimp, which are even more numerous.

## Out of sight

Most are born and raised in countries of the Global South (Ecuador, India, Vietnam, China…) before being exported to Europe, the United States or Japan. We never see the farms. On our shelves, shrimp arrive frozen, peeled, in plastic trays. Nothing reminds us they were living animals.

That distance hides other abuses too: investigations such as the Associated Press's in 2015 exposed forced labour in seafood supply chains.

## The worst practices

On many farms, breeding females undergo **eyestalk ablation**: one or both eyestalks are removed, without anaesthesia, to speed up egg-laying. At the end, many shrimp die of **asphyxiation** out of the water, or are plunged alive into ice-cold water. For animals [able to feel pain](../do-shrimp-feel-pain/), these practices are deeply cruel.

## Why supermarkets

Producing countries have few or no welfare standards for shrimp. The retailers who buy and sell them, however, have the power to demand better from their suppliers. Several European retailers have already committed. That's where we focus our pressure: on those who can change everything with one decision.
